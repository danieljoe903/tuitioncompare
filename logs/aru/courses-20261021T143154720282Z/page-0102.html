This applied Master's degree, co‑designed by ARU and the Eden Project, offers a unique blend of academic and experiential learning.

You can study full-time over 12 months, or part-time over 24 months providing the opportunity to study alongside your other responsibilities.

Delivered predominantly online, the programme includes five immersive residential weeks at ARU in Cambridge and the Eden Project in Cornwall. These in‑person experiences allow you to build strong connections with a close‑knit cohort, enriching your learning experience with diverse perspectives and real‑world insights.

You will learn from academics within our Global Sustainability Institute and engage directly with sustainability leaders from industry, civil society, government, and the third sector. You will apply your learning through your assignments and a professional placement, where you will also develop ‘Skills for Change’ – practical capabilities aligned with UNESCO’s Sustainability Competencies.

The programme’s flexibility allows you to dive deeply into a specific sustainability area or build a broad foundation across multiple themes.

With its focus on systems thinking, critical reflection, skills development and independent research this Master's equips you for a range of sustainability careers – whether you’re beginning your journey in the field, tackling sustainability challenges in your current role, or exploring a new professional direction.

Accredited by ISEP

This course is accredited by the Institute of Sustainability and Environmental Professionals (ISEP). This gives access to a global network of sustainability professionals, exclusive events, and toolkits and ensures that you graduate with industry-recognised skills in areas such as systems thinking, policy and legislation, environmental management tools, and leadership for change.

 

Study at an award-winning university

We were named Times Higher Education University of the Year 2023. We've also been awarded a Gold rating in the Teaching Excellence Framework – a reflection of the outstanding education we offer our students.

Careers

The course helped me realise my interests and led me to my new job [where] I am able to use the knowledge and skills gained from the MSc.

Sophie Bowers, MSc Sustainability graduate

People join the MSc Sustainability for many reasons, each leading to different career pathways.

You may want to deepen your sustainability expertise to strengthen your current role, or tackle long‑standing challenges in your organisation or sector.

Or you may be a recent graduate or early‑career professional who's eager to explore emerging opportunities in the sustainability field.

Examples of career roles after completing an MSc include:

  • Sustainability education lead in a further education college
  • Project manager
  • Sustainability consultant
  • Policy advisor
  • Research manager
  • Communications consultant
  • CSR manager
  • Further study, such as a research programme

Moving into a meaningful career

Since graduating from our MSc Sustainability degree, Sophie has moved sectors and now works in nature conservation with a wildlife trust.
  • This Masters programme is structured around UNESCO Sustainability Competencies, allowing to develop your professional competences whilst learning about sustainability through a systems-thinking lens.
  • It's ISEP-accredited, with core content designed to meet industry expectations for a sustainability qualification at this level.
  • You will complete a supported professional placement – either in your current organisation or a new one – to build connections and grow as a sustainability leader.
  • You will have access to employability services and an online Alumni Career Centre to support your next steps.

Teaching and modules

My favourite part has been the immersive experiences, whether at the Eden Project or the field trips at Cambridge. They bring all the learning to life in a really meaningful way.

Gayle Boulton, Head of Sustainability & Climate Change, Department for Education

Develop a strong foundation of academic knowledge while building personal competencies in systems thinking, independent research, and professionalism.

You'll start the course by establishing a solid grounding in sustainability from a systems perspective, helping you explore the interconnections between human and environmental systems and question prevailing societal assumptions. You will explore sustainability challenges using systems mapping techniques, whilst being introduced to academic methods and corporate sustainability tools that can support organisational decision making.

You'll also consider what leadership means for individuals and sectors, through a module developed in collaboration with the Eden Project.

This course includes a professional development placement – in your current workplace or a new organisation – where you'll reflect on your own capacity to lead and influence change.

Finally, you'll undertake an independent research project on a topic of your choice, supported by an academic supervisor. You can explore examples of previous students’ dissertations here, which demonstrate the scope of topics that have been pursued.

Module details

This course is delivered predominantly online. Sessions take place from 12-1pm on set days, designed to fit around your schedule.

There are also five residential weeks in Cambridge and Cornwall.

Your course includes five residential sessions. Three sessions take place at ARU in Cambridge, and two at the Eden Project in Cornwall.

Residential sessions are mandatory and dates are as follows.

Full-time course
You'll join five week-long residentials, two at the Eden Project and three in Cambridge. Your course lasts for 12 months and all residentials take place within that time.

Trimester 1, Cambridge:

  • 28 September- 2 October 2026
  • 19-23 October 2026
  • 23-27 November 2026

Trimester 2, Eden Project Cornwall:

  • 8-12 February 2027
  • 15-19 March 2027

Part-time course
You'll join five week-long residentials, two at the Eden Project and three in Cambridge. The residentials are split over two years.

Year 1, Trimester 1, Cambridge:

  • 28 September - 2 October 2o26
  • 23-27 November 2026

Year 1, Trimester 2, Eden Project Cornwall:

  • 8-12 February 2027
  • 15-19 March 2027

Year 2, Trimester 1, Cambridge:

  • October 2027, week-long, dates to be confirmed

Assessments take varied formats, providing the opportunity to develop a range of communication skills, including creating a sector specific future vision, producing a long-form academic essay, developing a business plan, writing reflective pieces, and completing an independent research dissertation.

Fees and funding

£12,275 UK students starting 2027/28 (full-time, per year)

Fees are payable upfront, in full or in instalments, but there's no need to pay until you've accepted an offer to study with us. Find out more about paying your fees and about postgraduate loans and funding.

£6,137 UK students starting 2027/28 (part-time, per year)

Fees are payable upfront, in full or in instalments, but there's no need to pay until you've accepted an offer to study with us. Find out more about paying your fees and about postgraduate loans and funding.

You will need to arrange and pay for travel, accommodation and subsistence costs associated with attending residentials, as they are not included in your course fee.

Part-time course fees assume that you’re studying at half the rate of a full-time student (50% intensity). Course fees will be different if you study over a longer period. All fees are for guidance purposes only. Your offer letter will contain full details of credits and fees, or you can contact us if you'd like more information.

If you already have an ARU degree, you may be eligible for our Alumni Scholarship. 

There's no need to apply (we'll automatically check eligibility), and you could save 20% on your tuition fees.

There are several ways to fund postgraduate study, and we recommend finding out more before you apply.
