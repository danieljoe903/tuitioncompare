This four-year degree at ARU in Writtle includes a Masters year, and combines theory with hands-on experience.

Using specialist equine and canine therapy centres, you’ll build expert skills in animal rehabilitation – from foundational knowledge to advanced clinical techniques.

Throughout the course, you'll assess, treat and rehabilitate animals using evidence-based practice. Clinical placements, research work and professional development will help prepare you for a career in veterinary physiotherapy.

Accredited course

Our course is accredited by the Register of Animal Musculoskeletal Practitioners (RAMP).

Why ARU?

  • Learn from expert tutors who are qualified veterinary physiotherapists and veterinary surgeons.

  • Use state-of-the-art equine and canine therapy centres, and work with real clinical cases.

  • Join an innovative four-year course, progressing to a Masters degree after three years.

  • Treat real patients in our commercial facilities, offering 1,900 appointments yearly – an educational journey unlike any other!

 

Study at an award-winning university

We were named Times Higher Education University of the Year 2023. We've also been awarded a Gold rating in the Teaching Excellence Framework – a reflection of the outstanding education we offer our students.

Careers

I have been able to [carry out] research into my own subjects of interest and learn a new skill that I can take with me on my journey to becoming a veterinary physiotherapist.

Adelaide Chinn, MVetPhys Veterinary Physiotherapy

Gain advanced clinical skills, business acumen and research experience – and graduate ready for a career in animal health.

Backed by professional accreditation, this course will prepare you for jobs in veterinary practices, rehabilitation centres, teaching, and emerging fields like digital health and performance technology.

With an MVetPhys in Veterinary Physiotherapy, you’ll be qualified for careers in the veterinary, animal health and rehabilitation sectors.

Most graduates become veterinary physiotherapists. In this role you'll assess, treat and rehabilitate animals, particularly dogs and horses. You might work independently or in a veterinary practice.

There are also opportunities in:

  • animal rehabilitation centres
  • welfare organisations
  • sports performance
  • research
  • teaching
  • consultancy. 

Graduation doesn't have to be the end of your time with us. You might decide to stay at ARU and join a research programme. Take advantage of our Alumni Scholarship and save £400 on your fees.

Teaching and modules

This course goes beyond textbooks, immersing you in hands-on learning that builds the confidence and expertise to excel as a veterinary physiotherapist while fostering personal growth and essential real-life skills.

Hope Jameson, Senior Lecturer in Veterinary Physiotherapy

Dive into the exciting world of animal anatomy, physiology, pathophysiology, and biomechanics.

You’ll explore essential topics such as nutrition, exercise science, pharmacology, disease management and rehabilitation. Gain hands-on experience in our canine and equine therapy centres, while building professional skills in research, business and ethics.

As you progress, you'll take on more independent learning and explore advanced therapeutic techniques, including massage, electrotherapy and remedial exercise. You’ll learn to apply scientific principles with confidence, preparing you for a successful career in clinical practice.

Module details

We’ll assess your progress in a number of ways, all aligned with industry requirements.

Assessments will include assignments, reports, written exams, observed structured practical exams, clinical evaluations, oral presentations, debates and your dissertation. Our diverse assessment approaches cater to different learning styles.

In your final year, your practical therapy skills are evaluated through an Observed Structured Clinical Exam (OSCE), ensuring you have essential Day One competency skills. Upon successful completion of exams and all academic components, you’ll be fully qualified to practice as a veterinary physiotherapist.

For this course, you’ll need a laptop with reliable internet access, and basic stationery like pens, pencils, notebooks or paper for notetaking, folders and a scientific calculator.

To take part in practical activities safely and comfortably, you’ll need a riding hat or skull cap that meets current BETA safety standards, riding gloves and sturdy yard boots, long or short. Clothing should be smart and practical, such as dark trousers, waterproofs, or plain jeans (no rips or embellishments). A warm, waterproof outdoor coat is also important, and a digital camera or phone can come in handy for recording your practical work. You may already have some of this equipment at home if you're used to handling horses. Estimated cost: £150–£250.

For canine hydrotherapy sessions, we recommend having your own waders to ensure comfort and a proper fit, plus a pair of Crocs. Estimated cost: £27–£45.

In Years 3 and 4, you’ll need to arrange individual insurance to cover clinical placements and early practice during your NAVP probation. We recommend Balens insurance, at a cost of £23.18 (2025).

During your final year, you’ll be working closely with equine or canine clients and the public. You'll need to wear professional attire, as follows. Costs are estimated.

Canine:

  • canine wetsuit or waders for hydrotherapy (£25–£50)
  • black trainers (no bright logos) or boots (£10–£60) for indoor use
  • black or navy t-shirt or long-sleeve top (£4–£20)
  • black or navy fleece (£15–£50)
  • black jeggings or jeans—no rips, blue rinse, or embellishments (£10–£30)
  • black or brown boots (£25–£150)

Equine:

  • black trainers (no coloured logos) or boots (£10-60) for indoor use
  • t-shirt or long sleeve top (black or navy) (£4-20)
  • fleece (black or navy) (£15-50)
  • trousers: black jeggings or jeans; no blue rinse jeans, rips or embellishments (£10-30)
  • black or brown boots for the yard (£25-£150)
  • as well as clothing, you'll need an equine stool for practicals (£10-20)

All clothing must be free of large logos or motifs; small, discreet branding is acceptable. Sleeveless tops, shorts, and hoodies are not permitted.

Our veterinary surgeons help to teach subjects such as anatomy, physiology and biomechanics. Meanwhile, our veterinary physiotherapists, who have extensive experience in equine hydrotherapy and canine rehabilitation, will be lecturing in rehabilitation, remedial exercise programmes and electrotherapy.

Entry requirements

Important additional notes

Our published entry requirements are a guide only and our decision will be based on your overall suitability for the course as well as whether you meet the minimum entry requirements.

Other equivalent qualifications may be accepted for entry to this course; please email [email protected] for further information. Applicants to undergraduate courses can also check our information about undergraduate entry qualifications.

You'll need a computer and reliable internet access to successfully engage with your course.

We don't accept AS level qualifications on their own for entry to our undergraduate degree courses. However for some degree courses a small number of tariff points from AS levels are accepted as long as they're combined with tariff points from A levels or other equivalent level 3 qualifications in other subjects.

Paying your tuition fees
If you're a UK student, you don't need the money to pay for university now.

If you accept an offer to study at university, you can apply for a Tuition Fee Loan to cover your course fees.

Both full-time and part-time students can apply for a Tuition Fee Loan. You'll start repaying the loan in the April after you graduate.

For more information about student loans, see our undergraduate student finance page.

This information also applies to:

  • Irish nationals living in the UK or Ireland
  • EU, EEA and Swiss nationals benefiting from citizens’ rights under the UK-EU Withdrawal Agreement.

Living at university

We understand that affordability is an important issue when you’re thinking about studying at university.

The overall cost of university can vary depending on where you’re studying and which course you’re taking.

Costs may also be lower if you're living at home. However, some of the main costs you’ll want to think about include:

  • accommodation
  • bills
  • internet, mobile phone and calls
  • travel
  • food and drink
  • socialising and going out
  • study materials.

Most full-time UK students can apply for a Maintenance Loan to help cover living costs. More about maintenance loans.

We also have a range of offers to help with the cost of living.

Our Money Advice Service is on hand if you’d like to chat about living costs, budgets and managing your money. You can also find out more about student finance at one of our Open Days.

Please see 'What equipment will I need?' in the Teaching and modules section above.

We offer a range of scholarships and bursaries for UK and international students, which provide extra financial support while you’re at university.

If you're eligible for a scholarship, you'll generally receive it automatically – there's no need to apply.

Our campus employment bureau, ARU Temps, can help you find part-time work that fits around your studies (including any time you may spend on placements). It's a great chance to enhance your CV and boost your income while you study.
