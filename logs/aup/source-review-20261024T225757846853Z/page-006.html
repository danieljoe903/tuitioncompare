Our postgraduate programmes invite you to explore and refine your creative practice by critically engaging with the cultural, technological and economic changes shaping our world. At Arts University Plymouth, you will study in a specialist arts university led by practising artists, designers and researchers, and join the Postgraduate Centre, an active, cross-disciplinary community of makers and thinkers.

<p>Whether you want to deepen your existing practice, change direction or accelerate your career, postgraduate study here gives you the time, space and critical support to develop a substantial body of work in Plymouth, a creative coastal city in the South West.</p>

At a glance

<ul class="postgraduate-list"> <li>Study a portfolio of MA courses across <strong>design</strong>, <strong>studio arts</strong>, <strong>media</strong> and <strong>craft</strong> disciplines</li> <li>Choose <strong>full-time</strong> study over one year or <strong>part-time</strong> study over two years</li> <li>Learn through a <strong>practice-led curriculum</strong> built around research methods, creative development and a final independent project</li> <li>Join the <strong>Postgraduate Centre</strong>, a dedicated cross-disciplinary community shared by MA students and alumni</li> <li>Learn from research-active staff, practising artists, designers and visiting experts</li> <li>Access specialist workshops, studios and technical support, alongside MIRROR, Plymouth Arts Cinema and Fab Lab South West</li> <li>Take part in Research Intensives, critiques, exhibitions and professional development opportunities</li> <li>Arts University Plymouth alumni can receive a 15% fee discount through the <strong>Alumni Progression Award</strong></li></ul>

Why choose Postgraduate study at Arts University Plymouth?

<p dir="ltr">Postgraduate study at Arts University Plymouth is designed for artists, designers, makers, writers, educators and creative practitioners who want to push their work further in a critical, practice-led environment. You might be building on a recent degree, returning to study after time in industry, or looking for a focused space in which to test ideas, expand your research and develop a clearer professional direction.</p> <p dir="ltr">Our MA courses bring together specialist study and cross-disciplinary exchange. You will develop your work within your chosen discipline while learning alongside students from different creative backgrounds, helping you see your own practice from fresh perspectives and situate it more clearly within contemporary culture.</p> <p dir="ltr">As a small specialist university, Arts University Plymouth offers a distinctive postgraduate experience: close staff contact, a strong sense of community, and a city-centre creative environment connected to Plymouth, the South West and wider professional networks.<br /></p>

What is the Postgraduate Centre?

<p dir="ltr">As an MA student at Arts University Plymouth you will join our Postgraduate Centre, an active, cross-disciplinary community of makers and thinkers. It is a dedicated base for postgraduate study, used by MA cohorts and alumni, and a space for discussion, making, peer exchange and reflective development.</p> <p dir="ltr">This shared environment is central to how postgraduate study works here. Studio conversations, seminars, critiques and self-directed study all contribute to a culture in which your ideas can be tested, refined and challenged in dialogue with others.</p> <p dir="ltr">The Postgraduate Centre also reflects one of the university’s key strengths: an arts university run by artists and designers, for artists and designers, where thinking and making are closely connected.<br /></p>

What and how will you study on an MA?

<p dir="ltr">Our postgraduate courses allow you to learn your chosen discipline through a practice-led curriculum that helps you encounter how it is currently understood and challenged within contemporary culture. You will learn by developing a body of work, informed by research methods that help you delve deeper into the theories, practices and professional contexts most relevant to your field.</p> <p dir="ltr">All MA courses are organised around three units of study:</p><ul><li dir="ltr"><p dir="ltr"><strong>Unit 701: Practice-Led Research Methods</strong><br />You will start to build an iterative body of written or studio-based work and be introduced to research-based skills and frameworks.</p></li><li dir="ltr"><p dir="ltr"><strong>Unit 702: Creative &amp; Critical Development</strong><br />You will put your research into action through participation in Research Intensives that expand your knowledge and understanding of your subject.</p></li><li dir="ltr"><p dir="ltr"><strong>Unit 703: Final Independent Project</strong><br />You will develop a final body of work or project, culminating in a public display or presentation.</p></li></ul> <p dir="ltr">The learning modality for MA study is a combination of taught content through lectures, critiques and tutorials and self-directed study. A full-time student would need to commit at least four days per week to MA study. A part-time student should commit at least two days per week to MA study, including one taught day and one self-directed study day.<br /></p>

What postgraduate courses can you study?

<p dir="ltr">Our portfolio of MA courses spans design disciplines and studio art, media and craft disciplines, giving you the opportunity to deepen your specialist practice within a multidisciplinary environment.</p> <p dir="ltr"><strong>Design-focused MA courses</strong></p><ul> <li dir="ltr">MA Design</li> <li dir="ltr">MA Design (Built Environment)</li> <li dir="ltr">MA Design (Communication)</li> <li dir="ltr">MA Design (Fashion)</li> <li dir="ltr">MA Design (Textiles)</li></ul> <p dir="ltr"><strong>Studio art, media and craft MA courses</strong></p><ul> <li dir="ltr">MA Craft &amp; Material Practices</li> <li dir="ltr">MA Fine Art</li> <li dir="ltr">MA Glass</li> <li dir="ltr">MA Immersive Media</li> <li dir="ltr">MA Museum Studies</li></ul> <p dir="ltr">MA courses in design share a concern for designing with planet and people in mind, while MA courses in studio arts, media and craft support the development of an independent voice through self-directed practice and critical reflection. Across both areas, the emphasis is on building a body of work that reflects your unique voice, skills and insights.<br /></p>

What facilities, research and opportunities will support your practice?

<p dir="ltr">Postgraduate students study in a research-intensive environment shaped by active makers and researchers. Alongside specialist workshops, studios and technical support, you will engage with a wider research culture through seminars, critiques, public talks and Research Intensives led by research-active staff and external specialists.</p> <p dir="ltr">You will also be in regular contact with <strong>Fab Lab South West</strong>, part of the international MIT Fab Lab network, <strong>Plymouth Arts Cinema</strong>, the only independent cinema in Plymouth, and <strong>MIRROR</strong>, our on-campus public gallery. Together, these spaces help connect your work to current conversations in contemporary art, design, media and material practice.</p> <p dir="ltr">Depending on your course and timing, postgraduate study can also open up opportunities including professional exhibitions, residencies, placements, fellowships, bursaries and research activity. These opportunities help you test your ideas beyond the taught curriculum and build confidence in presenting your work publicly and professionally.<br /></p>

How does postgraduate study connect to research and innovation?

<p dir="ltr">Arts University Plymouth is a centre of practice-led creative research where artists, designers and authors explore new ideas through making. As a postgraduate student, you study within this wider research environment and benefit from direct contact with emergent thinking in creative practice, creative education, material innovation and cross-disciplinary enquiry.</p> <p dir="ltr">This environment is shaped by staff-led research groups including <strong>Making Learning</strong>, <strong>Memory, Site, Artefact</strong> and <strong>Material Futures</strong>, as well as initiatives such as <strong>StudioLab for Embodied Media</strong>, the <strong>Centre for Circular Materials</strong> and the biennial <strong>Making Futures™</strong> conference.</p> <p dir="ltr">For students who want to deepen their practice, develop new methods or build towards future research, this creates a strong context for ambitious postgraduate study.<br /></p>

How can a creative MA help your career?

<p dir="ltr">Our postgraduate courses equip you with the agility to embrace rapid change and help you become a proactive, transformative leader in the creative and cultural industries. Through your studies, you will be supported to understand how your work is positioned, encountered and developed in relation to your longer-term ambitions.</p> <p dir="ltr">Your final MA project is presented in a professional context and is designed to prepare you to step into your career ready to work and thrive as an independent thinker and maker. During your time on the programme, you may also have opportunities to broaden your profile through exhibitions, peer critique, residencies, fellowships and bursaries.</p> <p dir="ltr">The university’s Careers and Enterprise team provides tools and workshops during your studies to help you plan your next steps after graduation, whether that means independent practice, portfolio work, further study, research, teaching or roles across the wider creative and cultural sectors.<br /></p>

How do you apply?

<p dir="ltr">To enrol on one of our MA courses, a first degree of at least a lower second class honours (2:2) in an art, design, craft or media discipline is normally required. We may also consider equivalent professional knowledge and demonstrated industry experience in individual cases.</p> <p dir="ltr">As part of your application, you will be asked to provide digital images of your work and submit a short Personal Statement and Project Proposal, each with a maximum of 300 words. The Project Proposal is your opportunity to tell us what you want to investigate on your chosen MA course, the ideas or practitioners that inspire you, and your longer-term personal and professional goals.</p> <p>You can apply via the <strong>Apply Now</strong> button on each course page. The best deadline to submit your MA application is <strong>1 July</strong>, although late applications may be considered until the start of the course if places are still available. If you are an international applicant, please visit our website and search your country for country-specific entry and English language guidance.</p>

FAQs

What is postgraduate study at Arts University Plymouth like?
<p>Postgraduate study at Arts University Plymouth is practice-led, cross-disciplinary and focused on helping you develop a substantial body of work through making, research and critical reflection. You will study within a specialist creative community centred on the Postgraduate Centre.<br /></p>
Can I study part-time?
<p>Yes. You can study full-time over one year or part-time over two years.<br /></p> <p> </p>
Do I need a 2:2 to apply?
<p>Applicants normally need a 2:2 or above in a related art, design, craft or media discipline. Equivalent professional knowledge and industry experience may also be considered in individual cases.<br /></p>
What do I need to submit with my application?
<p>You will usually need digital images of your work, a Personal Statement and a Project Proposal of up to 300 words.<br /></p>
Can I speak to someone before I apply?
<p>Yes. You can attend an Open Event, arrange a one-to-one pre-application advisory meeting or campus tour, or book an informal online chat about postgraduate study.<br /></p>
What facilities will I be able to use?
<p>Postgraduate students benefit from access to specialist workshops, studios, technical support and the wider postgraduate environment, including MIRROR, Plymouth Arts Cinema and Fab Lab South West.<br /></p>
Are there discounts for Arts University Plymouth alumni?
<p>Yes. Arts University Plymouth alumni can receive a 15% fee discount through the Alumni Progression Award.<br /></p>
What kinds of opportunities are available beyond the studio?
<p>Depending on your course and timing, opportunities may include exhibitions, critiques, residencies, placements, fellowships, bursaries and research activity that help you develop your work in professional contexts.<br /></p>
Where can I find course-specific information?
<p>You can explore each MA course in more detail on our postgraduate course pages, including course content, entry requirements and how to apply.</p>
